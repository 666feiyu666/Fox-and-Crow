# Writing craft for playable passages

## Attention and rhythm

Choose the detail that makes this response legible and specific. Filter attention
through what is available to the player character, without assigning a new
feeling or intention. An ordinary task can be summarised; a consequential moment
can unfold through action. Let sentence length follow the moment.

## Dialogue and ambiguity

Use the voice reference to distinguish speakers through vocabulary, rhythm and
what they avoid answering. Dialogue can express an authorised refusal without
explaining the speaker's private motive. Let unresolved tension remain unresolved.
A generic gesture repeated every turn will not make a character more particular.

## Scope

Work inside the brief's stopping point. Do not add reconciliation after an
argument or a lesson after failure simply to make a passage feel complete.
A quiet result is enough when the situation calls for it.

## Modes

- direct: give the result its simplest adequate expression.
- dialogue: let an exchange carry the authorised change.
- action: make the important attempt and its physical consequence comprehensible.
- transition: compress intervals with no intervening player decision.
- return: make recurrence and the remembered difference perceptible.
- ending: give the established consequence room to land.
