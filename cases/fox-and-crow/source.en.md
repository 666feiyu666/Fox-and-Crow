A crow, having found a piece of cheese, settled on a high branch to eat it in
peace. A fox passing below saw the cheese and wanted it. He could not climb, so he
stood under the tree and began to praise her: how glossy her feathers were, how
fine her shape, and how surely a bird so made must have a voice to match — the
queen of all birds, if she would only speak.

The crow, pleased to be praised, lifted her head and cawed. The cheese fell from
her beak. The fox ate it and went away laughing, saying that the crow had a voice
enough, and no wit at all beneath it.
