After his wife died, a gentleman married again — a proud, haughty woman with two
daughters of her own, alike in temper and in vanity. The work of the house fell to
his daughter: the washing, the mending, the fire, and the ashes. She slept in the
garret and was called Cinderella.

The king's son gave a ball. The stepsisters went in their finery, and Cinderella
was left with the dishes. Then her godmother came, turned a pumpkin into a coach
and mice into horses, dressed her, and told her plainly that at the last stroke of
midnight the magic would end. She went to the ball; the prince danced with her and
no one else; and at the stroke she ran, losing one glass slipper on the stairs.

The prince carried the slipper through the country. It fitted the stepsisters not
at all, and it fitted Cinderella exactly. She was taken to the palace and married
the prince.
